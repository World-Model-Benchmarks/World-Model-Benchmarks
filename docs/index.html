Overview

Evaluation claims need four coordinates.

Two benchmarks can report strong performance while supporting very different claims. The survey separates capability target, evaluation protocol, metric family, and data source so results can be interpreted on comparable terms.

01

Evaluation Target

Specifies the property of world-state evolution or downstream use being characterized.

02

Evaluation Protocol

Distinguishes open-loop evaluation from feedback-driven closed-loop interaction.

03

Evaluation Metrics

Separates prediction-level evidence from downstream outcome evidence.

04

Evaluation Data

Identifies whether benchmark cases come from real collection, simulation, curation, or hybrid construction.

Timeline

From “Does the generated world look reasonable?” to “Is it really useful to the agent?”

The corpus traces a shift from visual and temporal quality, physical plausibility, and causal and counterfactual reasoning toward spatial and state consistency, long-horizon memory and state persistence, control fidelity and interactive dynamics, and functional utility.

Four-phase conclusions

  1. 1

    Early benchmarks mainly focus on physical plausibility and causal reasoning.

    13 cumulative benchmarks by 2023, concentrated in physical and causal evaluation.

  2. 2

    From 2024 onward, control fidelity & interactive dynamics and functional utility begin to emerge.

    9 new benchmarks in 2024, including broader control and functional-utility evaluation.

  3. 3

    In 2025, benchmark growth accelerates, with broader coverage of spatial consistency, physical plausibility, and control fidelity.

    31 new benchmarks in 2025, raising the cumulative corpus to 53.

  4. 4

    By 2026, long-horizon memory & persistence and functional utility become prominent frontier directions.

    53 new benchmarks in 2026, bringing the corpus to 106.
